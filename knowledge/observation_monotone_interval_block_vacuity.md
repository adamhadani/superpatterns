Relevant to: Canonical skeletal decomposition, shared host squares, two-tier embedding strategies.

## Statement
In uniform random permutations $\pi \in S_k$ and in alternating permutations such as $21^{\oplus (k/2)}$, the length of the longest monotone interval block (contiguous positions mapping to contiguous values) is at most 2 or 3 with probability $1 - o(1)$ (and at most $O(\log k / \log\log k)$ in general). Consequently, for any macroscopic threshold $L \ge K\sqrt{\log k}$ or $L \ge \Theta(1/\varepsilon)$, the set of macroscopic monotone blocks is empty ($\mathcal{M} = \emptyset$) and the residual component is the entire permutation ($\mathcal{R} = [k]$).

## Evidence
1. Alternating permutations: For repeated-21 ($21^{\oplus m}$), every contiguous monotone block has length exactly 2 for all $k$.
2. Theory of simple permutations (Albert, Atkinson, Klazar 2003): Almost all permutations in $S_k$ are simple, containing no non-trivial interval blocks of length $\ge 2$ other than singletons and $[k]$.
3. Computational verification (Level 1 falsers 00, 02, 03, 08):
   - At $k=100$, 0 out of 1000 random permutations contained any monotone interval block of length $\ge 5$ (0.00% coverage).
   - At $k=1024$, the average maximum monotone interval block length in random permutations was $1.83$, with 100% of samples having maximum block length $\le 2 \le L$.
   - For threshold $L = \lceil 2\sqrt{\log k} \rceil = 6$, $|\mathcal{M}| = 0$ in 100% of tested trials across $k \in \{100, 500, 1000, 4096\}$.
4. Monotone sub-chain budgeting (Level 1 falser 11): In random permutations, the average length of a maximal monotone sub-chain is $2.42$ ($m_{\mathrm{res}} \approx 0.413k$), while for alternating permutations it is exactly $2.00$ ($m_{\mathrm{res}} = k/2$). Permutations admitting a partition into $m_{\mathrm{res}} \le \varepsilon k / 4$ monotone sub-chains (as required for $O(\varepsilon k)$ buffer budgeting) constitute less than $10^{-155}$ of $S_{100}$ at $\varepsilon=0.05$.
5. Combinatorial composition counting (Level 2 falser 04): The fraction of permutations in $S_k$ whose non-trivial monotone interval blocks cover at least an $\varepsilon$-fraction of elements is bounded by $\sum_{m \ge \varepsilon k} \binom{k}{m} 2^m (k-m)! / k! \le \exp(-\Theta(k \log k))$. At $k=100, \varepsilon=0.05$, the probability of having even one monotone block of length $\ge 5$ is $< 10^{-138.3}$, confirming that the macroscopic skeletal tier $\mathcal{M}$ is unconditionally empty for almost all permutations.
6. Level 3 Censuses on Generic Permutations (Level 3 falsers L03_N00, L03_N01, L03_N02, L03_N04): Across 1200+ independent random permutations across $k \in \{50, 100, 200, 500\}$, the mean maximum monotone interval block length was $1.86$ to $1.93$ (with maximum observed length 3). Exactly $0.0\%$ (0/1200) of trials contained any block of length $\ge L = \lceil K\sqrt{\log k}\rceil$, confirming that Tier 1 embeds $0$ points of generic permutations with probability $1 - o(1)$.
7. Level 4 Census and Vertical Span Overlap on Generic Permutations (Level 4 falser L04_N00): In a Monte Carlo census over 600 random permutations in $S_{100}$ and $S_{200}$, exactly $0.0\%$ (0/600) contained any monotone interval block of length $\ge 4$ (mean max block length $1.87$ to $1.89$), confirming that Tier 1 covers an asymptotically measure-zero subset ($\le 10^{-138}$ in $S_{100}$). Furthermore, horizontal halves have vertical spans overlapping by $95.95\%$ ($k=100$), $99.15\%$ ($k=500$), and $99.61\%$ ($k=1000$), directly refuting the disjoint value interval hypothesis of boundary-compatible gluing lemmas.
8. Extremal Coverage Void on Alternating Permutations (Round 2 Level 1 Aggregators L01_N01, L01_N05, L01_N07, L01_N13, L01_N15): For alternating permutations $21^{\oplus (k/2)}$, $L_2(\pi) = k-2 \gg 3\sqrt{k}$, strictly excluding them from the quasirandom class $\mathcal{Q}_k$. Simultaneously, every monotone interval block has length exactly 2, strictly excluding them from monotone inflations $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{infl}}$ (where blocks must satisfy $a \ge K\sqrt{\log k} \ge 4$). Hence alternating permutations fall into an unbridgeable coverage void between Tier 1 and Tier 2 of two-tier architectures, leaving them with zero containment mechanism.
9. Tier 1 Definition/Partition Mismatch and Spatial Overrun (Round 2 Level 3 falser L03_N04): In candidate L03_N04, Definition 5.1 defined Tier 1 as permutations possessing at least one monotone block of size $L_0 = \lceil K \sqrt{\log k}\rceil$. However, the embedding construction in Lemma 6.2 required that the permutation be *partitionable* into disjoint blocks of size $\ge L_0$, which occurred in exactly $0.0\%$ (0/5000) of random permutations in $S_{50}$. This creates a catastrophic definition-hypothesis mismatch, orphaning all generic permutations with isolated monotone runs. Furthermore, allocating guard corridors $\Delta$ between $m$ consecutive monotone square embeddings forces the total horizontal coordinate to expand as $1 + (m-1)\Delta/k > 1.0$, causing spatial coordinate overrun beyond the unit square host boundary.
10. **Tier 1 Monotone Run Value-Interleaving Defect and Measure-Zero Inflation Density (Round 2 Level 4 falser L04_N00)**: In candidate L04_N00, Theorem 6 embedded monotone blocks into candidate squares $Q_i$ with disjoint horizontal and vertical projections, which requires block values to form mutually disjoint intervals. However, Definition 5 defined $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{infl}}(\varepsilon)$ purely by domain partition into contiguous monotone blocks. Permutations with monotone runs but interleaved values (such as $\pi = (1, 3, 5, \dots, 2, 4, 6, \dots)$) satisfy Definition 5 yet cannot be embedded into disjoint host squares, leaving them completely orphaned. Furthermore, the true class of interval inflations $\tau[B_1, \dots, B_m]$ with blocks $\ge L_0$ has superexponentially small density in $S_k$, bounded by $|\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{infl}}| / k! \le 10^{-155.1}$ at $k=100$, $\le 10^{-1133.8}$ at $k=500$, and $\le 10^{-2560.2}$ at $k=1000$, proving that Tier 1 covers an asymptotically measure-zero fraction of $S_k$.
11. **$D_4$ Inversion Duality Preserves Interval Block Sizes Identically (Round 3 Level 1 N04)**: Transposition $\pi \leftrightarrow \pi^{-1}$ reflects the permutation diagram along the main diagonal, swapping domain intervals with value intervals. Because true interval blocks require both domain and range to be contiguous intervals, a block is an interval block in $\pi$ if and only if it is an interval block in $\pi^{-1}$, making $|\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{int}}(\pi^{-1})| \equiv |\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{int}}(\pi)|$ identically. Duality creates exactly zero new interval blocks and cannot convert interleaved runs into interval inflations.

Furthermore, multi-slope interleaved permutations (e.g. 500 increasing odd values followed by 500 decreasing even values) have $\operatorname{LIS} = 501$ and $\operatorname{LDS} = 500$, refuting the claim that contiguous monotone runs imply bounded Dilworth chain width $d = O(1)$.
13. **Round 3 Level 4 Master Architecture Census and Exact Composition Scope (explore_merger_r3_L04_N00, explore_falser_r3_L04_N00)**: By exact combinatorial composition counting, exactly 44,218 permutations in $S_{1000}$ qualify for Tier 1 with block threshold $L_0 = 320$, representing a fraction $\le 10^{-2562.96}$ of $S_{1000}$ (an asymptotically measure-zero set by Albert, Atkinson, Klazar 2003 simple permutation density $1 - o(1) \to 1.0$). Furthermore, multi-slope interleaved permutations with contiguous monotone domain runs have overlapping value spans ($\operatorname{LIS}=501, \operatorname{LDS}=500$ at $k=1000$), suffering an $85\%$ domain inversion in concurrent diagonal ribbons and remaining completely orphaned from Tier 1.

## Implications
Strategies that rely on canonical skeletal decomposition $\pi = \mathcal{M} \cup \mathcal{R}$ to absorb the bulk of the target permutation into a polynomial family of shared host squares $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{squares}}$ embed 0% of elements for generic and alternating targets ($|\mathcal{M}| = 0$, $|\mathcal{R}| = k$). This collapses the entire target permutation into the residual component $\mathcal{R}$, where fine-scale discrete buffer penalties and entropy barriers remain completely unmitigated.

## Caveats
This vacuity holds for generic and unstructured permutations. It does not hold for structured monotone inflations (such as permutations constructed with blocks of size $a \ge K\sqrt{\log k}$ as in W39), where $\mathcal{M} = [k]$ by definition.
