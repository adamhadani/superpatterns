# Discrete Rank-Slot Integer Buffer Granularity Barrier

Type: observation
Confidence: high
Source: Candidates 0, 1, 3, 4, 7 (Level 0 falsers); Mergers 00, 01, 02, 08, 09, 11, 12, 14, 15 (Level 1 falsers); Candidates L02_N00, L02_N02, L02_N06 (Level 2 falsers); Candidates L03_N00, L03_N01, L03_N04 (Level 3 falsers); Candidate L04_N00 (Level 4 falser)
Relevant to: Lookahead buffers, discrete embedding, boundary discretization penalties, Greene-Dilworth chain decompositions.

## Statement
In any discrete coordinate embedding of permutations into planar point sets, preventing coordinate collisions and rank inversions across block or chain boundaries requires reserving an absolute coordinate separation of at least 1 discrete rank slot and at least 1 integer host point per interface. Consequently, across $2^j$ interfaces at dyadic scale $j$, the discrete buffer penalty is at least $2^j \times 1 = 2^j$, which grows exponentially with scale $j$ and sums to $\ge k/2 = \Omega(k)$ across fine scales, strictly exceeding the hydrodynamic surplus $2\varepsilon k$ for all $\varepsilon < 1/4$. Furthermore, decomposing into Greene-Dilworth monotone chains does NOT bypass this barrier: poset chains are interleaved subsequences that transition $\Theta(k)$ times ($> 96\%$ of adjacent positions in random permutations, $100\%$ in alternating permutations), incurring $\Theta(k)$ discrete buffer penalties that overwhelm surplus.

## Evidence
1. Analytical evaluation: To ensure $x(p_t) < x(p_{t+1})$ and $y(p_{\pi^{-1}(v)}) < y(p_{\pi^{-1}(v+1)})$ deterministically, the buffer strip must not contain conflicting host points, costing at least $\Delta \ge 1$ rank slots.
2. Sum of discrete cuts: Across fine scales $j > j^*(\varepsilon)$ down to $J = \lceil\log_2 k\rceil$, the number of fine-scale cuts is $\sum_{j=j^*+1}^J 2^{j-1} = k - 2^{j^*}$. For $k \ge 2^{j^*+1}$, this is at least $k/2$.
3. Net drift deficit: $D_{\mathrm{net}} \le 2\varepsilon k - k/2 = (2\varepsilon - 1/2)k < 0$ for all $\varepsilon < 1/4$.
4. Poset chain transition frequency (Level 1 falsers 00, 08): For uniform random permutations at $k=1024$, adjacent indices transition between different Dilworth chains on average $984.4$ times ($96.1\%$), requiring $\approx 984$ discrete buffer slots. For alternating permutations $21^{\oplus (k/2)}$, transitions occur at $100\%$ of steps ($k-1$ transitions). The resulting discrete penalty is $\Theta(k)$, driving net drift to $-907.6$ at $k=1024, \varepsilon=0.10$.
5. Mesoscopic internal descents (Level 1 falser 01): Terminating dyadic cuts at mesoscopic scale $a_{\min} = \Theta(1/\varepsilon)$ leaves $k/2$ internal descents within terminal blocks for alternating/random permutations, which still require $\ge 0.5k$ discrete buffer slots.
6. Poisson void trapping in fine rank-slot windows (Level 1 falser 12): Flexible rank-slot windows $W_t^{\mathrm{flex}}$ of area $\le 1/k^2$ have Poisson mean $\mu \le 0.275$, empty with probability $\ge 76\%$. Across $k$ independent windows, the probability that all are non-empty is $\le (0.24)^k \le 10^{-63}$ at $k=100$, demonstrating that discrete point starvation is virtually certain without macroscopic coordinate slack.
7. Effective velocity degradation (Level 1 falser 15): Due to $\ge k/2$ descent boundaries requiring discrete buffer spacing, effective embedding velocity drops to $v_{\mathrm{eff}} \le 0.73 < 1.0$ at $\varepsilon = 0.05$, rendering traversal strongly subcritical.
8. Erdős-Szekeres chain count barrier (Level 2 falsers 00, 02, 06): Decomposing the residual $\mathcal{R}$ into Dilworth chains cannot reduce discrete buffer count to $o(k)$, because by Erdős-Szekeres, any permutation of length $k$ has $\operatorname{LIS}(\pi) \times \operatorname{LDS}(\pi) \ge k$. For alternating permutations $21^{\oplus (k/2)}$, $\operatorname{LIS} = k/2$ and $\operatorname{LDS} = 2$, but for reversed or unstructured blocks, $\operatorname{LDS}(\mathcal{R}) \ge \sqrt{|\mathcal{R}|} \ge \sqrt{k} \gg K\sqrt{\log k}$, requiring at least $\sqrt{k}$ chains and $\ge \sqrt{k}$ discrete boundaries.
9. Shrunken square corridor deficit (Level 2 falser 06): Reserving boundary guard corridors of width $1$ slot shrinks effective mesoscopic squares from $a$ to $a-2$, creating an absolute capacity deficit for all $a < 21.5$ at $\varepsilon=0.05$.
10. Level 3 Net Drift Bounds (Level 3 falsers L03_N00, L03_N01, L03_N04): At host intensity $(1/4+\varepsilon/2)k^2$, gross continuous surplus is $(\sqrt{1+4\varepsilon}-1)k \le 0.0954k$ at $\varepsilon=0.05$ ($0.0488k$ under thinning at $\varepsilon/2$). Reserving $\ge 1$ integer slot per cut incurs $P_{\mathrm{disc}} \ge 0.413k$ (random) to $0.500k$ (alternating), forcing net drift strictly negative ($D_{\mathrm{net}} \le -0.364k$ to $-0.451k < 0$) for all $\varepsilon \le 0.20$. Achieving positive net drift under integer buffers mathematically requires $\varepsilon > 0.498$, refuting supercritical renewal drift for all small $\varepsilon$.
11. Level 4 Renewal Drift Falsification (Level 4 falser L04_N00): In the synthesized master architecture, evaluating the discrete buffer-amortized renewal drift across $\varepsilon \in [0.01, 0.20]$ proved that discrete buffer penalties ($0.4133$ to $0.5000$) strictly overwhelm continuous surplus ($+0.0100$ to $+0.0488$ at $\varepsilon=0.05$) by $8\times$ to $10\times$. This forces net forward traversal drift deeply negative ($D_{\mathrm{net}} \le -0.3645k < 0$) and drops effective velocity to $v_{\mathrm{eff}} \le 0.7303 < 1.0$ at $\varepsilon=0.05$, refuting the primary route's core mechanism.

## Implications
Attempts to model discrete boundary penalties as continuously decaying fractions $\delta_j = O(2^{-j/2}/k)$ violate integer point reality at fine scales where $k \delta_J = O(k^{-1/2}) \ll 1$. Similarly, attempts to amortize buffers across Dilworth chains fail because interleaved chains require coordinate separation at every transition, incurring $\Omega(k)$ discrete buffer losses. Discrete lookahead cannot decouple microscopic elements without paying $\Omega(k)$ buffer losses.

## Caveats
Continuous hydrodynamic coupling avoids integer buffer penalties only when blocks are macroscopic ($a \gg 1$), where buffer spacing $O(1)$ is negligible compared to block capacity $2\sqrt{a}$.
