# Multi-Line Hammersley Virtual Coordinates and Non-Monotonicity Obstruction

Type: observation
Confidence: high
Source: explorer_r2_L00_N24 (Level 0)
Relevant to: Multi-line Hammersley process, interacting particle systems, Robinson--Schensted--Knuth correspondence, non-local poset embeddings

## Statement
In attempts to embed target permutation posets into planar Poisson point processes $\Pi_{n_0}$ via the continuous multi-line Hammersley interacting particle process (Aldous and Diaconis 1995), selecting points from higher Hammersley lines $m \ge 1$ fails to produce valid induced subpatterns of $\Pi_{n_0}$. While line 0 processes actual points of $\Pi_{n_0}$, lines $m \ge 1$ receive displaced particle heights $y^*$ at arrival times $x$. The resulting coordinate pairs $(x, y^*)$ are virtual/chimeric coordinates that do not belong to the host point process $\Pi_{n_0}$ (empirically $0.0\%$ of line $m \ge 1$ coordinates exist in $\Pi_{n_0}$). Furthermore, the sequence of emitted points along line $m \ge 1$ exhibits frequent coordinate descents (non-increasing trajectories), failing to trace increasing chains.

## Evidence
- Candidate explorer_r2_L00_N24 asserted in Theorem 5.1 that each line $m$ of the multi-line Hammersley process traces a disjoint increasing chain of points in $\Pi_{n_0}$.
- Algorithmic simulation of the multi-line Hammersley interacting particle process on 50 planar Poisson points across 4 coupled lines confirmed:
  1. Points on line $m \ge 1$ consist of virtual heights $y^*$ generated by particle displacement; exactly $0/37$ ($0.0\%$) on line 1, $0/27$ ($0.0\%$) on line 2, and $0/19$ ($0.0\%$) on line 3 exist in the original Poisson host $\Pi_{n_0}$.
  2. Trajectories along line $m \ge 1$ contain numerous inversions (10 to 22 descents per line), violating path monotonicity.
- Although line lengths $|Y^{(m)}(1)| = \lambda_m(\Pi_{n_0})$ correctly realize RSK Young tableau row lengths, the particles do not constitute a physical chain of points in $\Pi_{n_0}$.

## Implications
- The multi-line Hammersley process cannot be used as a physical point-selection engine to extract multiple disjoint increasing chains from a Poisson point process.
- Greene's theorem characterizes only the numerical cardinality of maximal chain unions, but the interacting particle lines that compute these invariants do not lie in the host point set.
- Any poset embedding based on multi-chain decompositions must select actual points of $\Pi_{n_0}$ directly rather than virtual outputs of particle displacement lines.

## Caveats
- Line 0 of the Hammersley process consists entirely of real points of $\Pi_{n_0}$, but traces only the longest increasing subsequence (LIS, $\lambda_1$). The virtuality failure occurs specifically on lines $m \ge 1$.
