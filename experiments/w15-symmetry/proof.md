## 0. The dihedral group and horizontal chains

Let D be the group of order 8 generated by the three involutions on S_k
  reverse    π^r(i) := π(k−1−i),     complement  π^c(i) := k−1−π(i),     inverse  π^ι := π^{-1}.
(These are the symmetries of the diagram {(i, π(i))} ⊆ [k]²: reflection in a vertical axis, in a
horizontal axis, and in the diagonal.)  The same maps act on S_n.

**Fact 0 (containment is D-equivariant).**  For σ ∈ S_n, π ∈ S_k and g ∈ D: σ contains π ⟺ σ^g
contains π^g.  *Proof.*  It suffices to check the generators.  σ contains π iff there are positions
i_0 < ⋯ < i_{k−1} with (σ(i_j))_j order-isomorphic to (π(j))_j.  Reverse: the positions
n−1−i_{k−1} < ⋯ < n−1−i_0 carry in σ^r the reversed sequence, order-isomorphic to π^r.  Complement:
the same positions carry n−1−σ(i_j), order-isomorphic to π^c.  Inverse: the point set
{(i_j, σ(i_j))} is order-isomorphic (as a subset of the plane, in both coordinates) to the diagram of π;
its transpose is a subset of the diagram of σ^{-1}, order-isomorphic to the transposed diagram of π,
i.e. to π^{-1}.  Each generator is an involution, so ⟸ follows from ⟹.  ∎

**Vertical and horizontal chains.**  For Δ ≥ 1 the Δ-shift chains of [W9]/[HK] (sets A ⊆ [k] on
which b(a) := π^{-1}(π(a) − Δ) is defined and increasing) will be called *vertical* Δ-chains, with
L_Δ(π) = L^V_Δ(π) their maximum size.  A set X ⊆ {Δ, …, k−1} of positions is a *horizontal*
Δ-chain of π if the two maps x ↦ π(x) and x ↦ π(x − Δ) are comonotone on X (for x, x' ∈ X:
π(x) < π(x') ⟺ π(x−Δ) < π(x'−Δ)); L^H_Δ(π) := max |X|.  (Vertical chains pair points of the
diagram at vertical distance Δ, consistently ordered by position; horizontal chains pair points at
horizontal distance Δ, consistently ordered by value.)

## 1. How chains and their LDS/LIS transform

**Lemma 1.**  Let Δ ≥ 1, A ⊆ [k], and let b = b_{π,Δ}.
 (a) (complement) A is a vertical Δ-chain of π ⟺ b(A) is a vertical Δ-chain of π^c, and
     (π^c(b(a)))_{a∈A} = (k−1+Δ − π(a))_{a∈A}; hence LDS(π^c|_{b(A)}) = LIS(π|_A) and
     LIS(π^c|_{b(A)}) = LDS(π|_A).
 (b) (reverse) A is a vertical Δ-chain of π ⟺ k−1−A is a vertical Δ-chain of π^r, and
     LDS(π^r|_{k−1−A}) = LIS(π|_A), LIS(π^r|_{k−1−A}) = LDS(π|_A).
 (c) (inverse) V ⊆ [k] is a vertical Δ-chain of π^{-1} ⟺ X := π^{-1}(V) is a horizontal Δ-chain
     of π, and LDS(π^{-1}|_V) = LDS(π|_X), LIS(π^{-1}|_V) = LIS(π|_X).
Consequently L_Δ(π^g) = L^V_Δ(π) for g ∈ {id, r, c, rc} and L_Δ(π^g) = L^H_Δ(π) for
g ∈ {ι, ιr, ιc, ιrc}; and, writing ρ^{V↓}(π) := max_Δ max_A LDS(π|_A) over vertical Δ-chains A,
ρ^{V↑} the same with LIS, and ρ^{H↓}, ρ^{H↑} the same over horizontal chains X, the quantity
ρ(π^g) := max_Δ max_{A vertical Δ-chain of π^g} LDS(π^g|_A) of [W13] Corollary 6 equals

   g:      id, rc → ρ^{V↓}(π);     r, c → ρ^{V↑}(π);     ι, ιrc → ρ^{H↓}(π);     ιr, ιc → ρ^{H↑}(π).

*Proof.*  (a) For a ∈ A put β = b(a), so π(β) = π(a) − Δ.  Then
b_{π^c,Δ}(β) = (π^c)^{-1}(π^c(β) − Δ) = (π^c)^{-1}(k−1−π(a)) = a.  Thus b_{π^c,Δ} is defined on
b(A) and is the inverse bijection of b: A → b(A); it is increasing iff b is.  So A is a chain of π
iff b(A) is a chain of π^c.  The value sequence is π^c(b(a)) = k−1−π(a)+Δ, which is the complement
of (π(a))_a listed in the same order (b is increasing), so its LDS/LIS are the LIS/LDS of (π(a))_a.
(b) π^r(k−1−a) = π(a) and b_{π^r,Δ}(k−1−a) = (π^r)^{-1}(π(a) − Δ) = k−1−b(a).  Listing k−1−A in
increasing order lists A in decreasing order, and k−1−b(a) increases as a decreases iff b(a)
increases with a.  The value sequence of π^r on k−1−A is (π(a))_a in decreasing order of a, i.e.
the reverse of π|_A, whose LDS is LIS(π|_A).
(c) Let V be listed increasingly and put x_v := π^{-1}(v).  V is a vertical Δ-chain of π^{-1} iff
b'(v) := π(π^{-1}(v) − Δ) = π(x_v − Δ) is defined (x_v ≥ Δ) and increasing in v.  Listing V
increasingly lists X = {x_v} by increasing π(x); so V is a chain iff π(x − Δ) increases along X
ordered by π(x), i.e. iff π(·) and π(· − Δ) are comonotone on X.  The sequence (π^{-1}(v))_{v∈V}
= (x_v) listed by increasing value is the inverse of the pattern of π|_X, and a pattern and its
inverse have the same LDS and LIS (a decreasing subsequence is a set of points pairwise in
NW–SE position, a property symmetric under transposition; likewise for increasing).
The consequences follow by composing: e.g. for g = ιc apply (a) to π^{-1} and then (c).  ∎

**Lemma 2 (invariance).**  Let σ be uniform in S_n, 𝒞 ⊆ S_k and g ∈ D.  Then
Pr(σ contains every π ∈ 𝒞^g) = Pr(σ contains every π ∈ 𝒞).
*Proof.*  By Fact 0, σ contains every π ∈ 𝒞^g iff σ^{g^{-1}} contains every π ∈ 𝒞, and σ^{g^{-1}}
is uniform in S_n.  ∎

Note that the random *matrix* M of [W9]/[W13] is invariant under row and column reversal but not
under transposition (q = 2k ≠ m); the inverse symmetry is available only at the level of σ (V-threads
on M, [W9] §1, are not the same thing and are useless there).  Lemma 2 costs nothing: no per-π union
bound needs to be redone, the whole event is transported.

## 2. The best-symmetry theorem

**Theorem 3.**  Let 𝒞_k ⊆ S_k be any class such that a uniformly random σ ∈ S_n contains every
π ∈ 𝒞_k w.h.p.  Then w.h.p. σ contains every π ∈ ⋃_{g∈D} 𝒞_k^g.
*Proof.*  Pr(∃ g, π ∈ 𝒞_k^g : σ ⊅ π) ≤ Σ_{g∈D} Pr(∃ π ∈ 𝒞_k : σ ⊅ π) = 8·o(1) by Lemma 2.  ∎

Applied to [W13] Theorem 5 (n = 800k²), [W9] Theorem 8 (n = 72k²) and Theorem 8 below, Lemma 1
makes the eight transported hypotheses explicit.  For [W13] Theorem 5 write
F^V(π; r, ℓ) := {Δ ∈ [k] : L^V_Δ(π) > L(r,ℓ)} and F^H(π; r, ℓ) := {Δ : L^H_Δ(π) > L(r,ℓ)}.

**Corollary 4 (explicit list, n = 800k²).**  W.h.p. a uniformly random σ ∈ S_{800k²} contains every
π ∈ S_k satisfying one of the following, for some r ≤ k/(2 ln(k+1)) in (i)–(ii) and some r ≤ k in (iii):
 (i)  (value-interval runs, vertical condition)  the values [k] split into ≤ r intervals on each of which
      π^{-1} is increasing [π ∈ 𝓛_r], or on each of which π^{-1} is decreasing [π ∈ 𝓛_r^c = 𝓛_r^r];
      and ℓ_r(2|F^V(π; r, ℓ_r)| + 1) ≤ k;
 (ii) (position-interval runs, horizontal condition)  the positions [k] split into ≤ r intervals on each of
      which π is increasing [π ∈ 𝓛_r^ι, i.e. π has ≤ r ascending runs] or on each of which π is
      decreasing [π ∈ 𝓛_r^{ιc}]; and ℓ_r(2|F^H(π; r, ℓ_r)| + 1) ≤ k;
 (iii) LDS(π) ≤ r or LIS(π) ≤ r, and ℓ'_r(2|F(π; r, ℓ'_r)| + 1) ≤ k for F = F^V *or* F = F^H.
In particular every π with ≤ e^{28} runs in any of the four senses is contained unconditionally.
*Proof.*  (i): 𝓛_r^c = {π : (π^c)^{-1} increasing on value intervals} = {π^{-1} decreasing on
value intervals} (complementing values reverses each interval); 𝓛_r^r is the same class since
(π^r)^{-1}(v) = k−1−π^{-1}(v).  The quasirandomness hypothesis of [W13] Theorem 5 for π^c and π^r is
the vertical one for π by Lemma 1(a,b) (same chain lengths).  (ii): π^{-1} ∈ 𝓛_r iff π is increasing
on ≤ r position intervals; its hypothesis is on L_Δ(π^{-1}) = L^H_Δ(π) by Lemma 1(c); the class
𝓛_r^{ιc} = (𝓛_r^c)^ι similarly.  (iii): 𝓓_r is closed under ι (LDS(π^{-1}) = LDS(π)), 𝓓_r^c =
{LIS ≤ r}; the hypothesis for π^ι is horizontal.  Apply Theorem 3 to [W13] Theorem 5.  ∎

(The LDS/LIS of chain restrictions do not enter [W13] Theorem 5, whose ρ is the global r; they enter
[W13] Corollary 6, where by Lemma 1 the eight images give the four values ρ^{V↓}, ρ^{V↑}, ρ^{H↓},
ρ^{H↑} listed above.  Since Corollary 6 tolerates ρ = k at the cost of the [W9] threshold
k/(3 ln² k), the substance of the symmetric condition is the pair of chain profiles (L^V_Δ), (L^H_Δ),
not the LDS/LIS values.)

## 3. Mixed monotone chains: the staircase count with both directions

The count of [W13] Lemma 1 extends to chains of either direction, which removes the need to choose
between π and π^c in [W13] and covers unions of monotone pieces of *mixed* directions.

**Definition.**  A *mixed ρ-staircase set* is a set S of cells of [q] × [m] with pairwise distinct rows
and columns which is a union of ≤ ρ chains, each chain being *increasing* (rows increase with columns)
or *decreasing* (rows decrease as columns increase).  𝓑^±_{L,s,ρ} is the event: for every mixed
ρ-staircase set S with |S| ≤ L, Σ_{c∈S} r(c) < s.
𝓜_r := {π ∈ S_k : [k] is a union of ≤ r sets on each of which π is monotone};
𝓛^±_r := {π : the values split into ≤ r intervals V_j, on each of which π^{-1} is monotone}.
(𝓜_r ⊇ 𝓓_r ∪ 𝓓_r^c, 𝓛^±_r ⊇ 𝓛_r ∪ 𝓛_r^c; both new classes are closed under r and c, and 𝓜_r under ι.)

**Lemma 5 (count).**  The number of mixed ρ-staircase sets of size j is ≤ C(q,j) C(m,j) ρ^{2j} 2^{min(ρ,j)},
and |𝓜_r| ≤ r^{2k} 2^r, |𝓛^±_r| ≤ (k+1)^r r^k 2^r.
*Proof.*  As in [W13] Lemma 1: after choosing rows and columns, S is a permutation σ ∈ S_j; a
partition of [j] into ≤ ρ' classes (ρ' := min(ρ, j); if ρ > j, singletons suffice) on each of which σ is
monotone is encoded by labelling positions and values with their class (ρ'^{2j} ways) and each class
with its direction (2^{ρ'} ways); within a class the matching of positions to values is forced (in
increasing or decreasing order).  The bound for 𝓜_r is the case j = k, q = m = k.  For 𝓛^±_r choose the
interval sizes ((k+1)^r), the direction of each interval (2^r), and for each position its interval
(r^k); the values are then forced.  ∎

**Lemma 6.**  Pr(¬𝓑^±_{L,s,ρ}) ≤ L (2e² q m ρ² / L²)^L e^{−s/8} if s ≥ 4L and L ≤ k.
*Proof.*  As [W13] Lemma 2 with the count of Lemma 5 and 2^{min(ρ,j)} ≤ 2^j: the summand is
f^±(j) := (2e²qmρ²/j²)^j, whose logarithm has derivative ln(2e²qmρ²/j²) − 2 ≥ 0 for j ≤ √(2qm)ρ,
and L ≤ k ≤ √(2qm).  ∎

**Lemma 7.**  If thread t reaches the elements of A and π|_A is a union of ≤ ρ monotone subsequences,
then the start cells {X_a : a ∈ A} form a mixed ρ-staircase set.  In particular this holds with ρ = r
for every A if π ∈ 𝓜_r, and for π ∈ 𝓛^±_r with the chains {X_a : a ∈ A, π(a) ∈ V_j}.  The same holds for
the modified procedure of [W9] Theorem 7.
*Proof.*  As [W13] Lemma 3: the columns of the X_a increase with a and the row of X_a is π(a)+t; a
subsequence of A on which π is increasing (decreasing) gives an increasing (decreasing) chain.  For
π ∈ 𝓛^±_r and a < a' with π(a), π(a') ∈ V_j: π(a) < π(a') if π^{-1} is increasing on V_j and π(a) > π(a')
if it is decreasing.  ∎

**Theorem 8 (= [W13] Theorem 4 and 5 with mixed directions).**  (a) [W13] Theorem 4 holds verbatim with
𝓑^±_{L,s,ρ} in place of 𝓑_{L,s,ρ} and the hypothesis "LDS(π|_A) ≤ ρ" replaced by "π|_A is a union of ≤ ρ
monotone subsequences".  (b) Let m = 100k, q = 2k, and with λ(r,ℓ), L(r,ℓ), ℓ_r, ℓ'_r, F(π; r, ℓ) as in
[W13] §4 put s^±(r,ℓ) := 8 L(r,ℓ)(ln(4e²·100·r²λ(r,ℓ)²) + 2) and
𝓜_r^{qr}(ℓ) := {π ∈ 𝓜_r : ℓ(2|F(π;r,ℓ)| + 1) ≤ k}.  For k large, w.h.p. M contains every π in
   ⋃_{r ≤ k/(2 ln(k+1))} (𝓛^±_r ∩ 𝓜_r^{qr}(ℓ_r))  ∪  ⋃_{r ≤ k} 𝓜_r^{qr}(ℓ'_r),
hence w.h.p. σ ∈ S_{800k²} contains every such π, and (Theorem 3) every dihedral image of such π.
*Proof.*  (a) The proof of [W13] Theorem 4 uses only: (i)–(ii) the pretend mechanism for the event
(same argument with "mixed ρ-staircase" in rule (b)), and (iii) that the leader's start cells along a
shift chain form a set controlled by the event, which is Lemma 7.
(b) Repeat the proof of [W13] Theorem 5 with these changes.  (Arithmetic.)  With K^± := 4e²·100·r²λ²
= 2K, Lemma 6 gives Pr(¬𝓑^±_{L,s^±,r}) ≤ L (K^±)^L e^{−L(ln K^± + 2)} = L e^{−2L} ≤ e^{−L}.  Next
8 ln K^± + 17 = 8 ln(400e²) + 16 ln r + 16 ln λ + 17 ≤ 16 ln r + 16 ln λ + 81 (8 ln(400e²) ≤ 63.94), and
the claim 49λ ≥ (ℓ−1)(16 ln r + 16 ln λ + 81) reduces, after dividing by ℓ−1 and using
λ = (ℓ−1)(ln r+10) ≤ (ln r + 10)², to 33 ln r + 409 ≥ 32 ln(ln r + 10), true for all r ≥ 1 (at r = 1 the
right side is 73.7; its derivative in ln r is ≤ 3.2).  Hence (ℓ−1)(s^± + L) ≤ 49k + (ℓ−1)(16 ln r +
16 ln λ + 81) ≤ 49k + 100 ln² k ≤ 50k = m/2 for k large, and s^± ≥ 4L.  (Global events.)  As in [W13]
(b): Pr(¬⋂𝓑^±) ≤ 2k e^{−k/ln² k} → 0.  (One pattern.)  As in [W13] (c) using (a) with ρ = r: on the
global event Pr(M ⊅ π) ≤ e^{−29ℓk}.  (Union bound.)  First family, by Lemma 5:
Σ_{r ≤ k/(2 ln(k+1))} (k+1)^r r^k 2^r e^{−k(ln r + 1)} ≤ Σ_r e^{r ln(2(k+1)) − k} ≤ k e^{−k/2 + k ln 2/(2 ln(k+1))}
→ 0.  Second family: Σ_{r ≤ k} r^{2k} 2^r e^{−k(2 ln r + 1)} ≤ 2^{k+1} e^{−k} → 0.  ∎

So at n = 800k² every union of ≤ r monotone runs on value intervals (or, by Theorem 3, on position
intervals: every concatenation of ≤ r monotone segments), each run increasing or decreasing at will, is
contained for r ≤ e^{28} unconditionally, and for larger r under the vertical (resp. horizontal)
quasirandomness condition; likewise every union of ≤ r monotone subsequences under vertical *or*
horizontal quasirandomness.

## 4. The residual class: a doubly structured family that defeats all eight images

**Definition.**  Let k = rh.  For τ = (τ_0, …, τ_{h−1}) ∈ (S_r)^h define π_τ ∈ S_k by

   π_τ(i·r + s) := τ_i(s)·h + i        (i ∈ [h], s ∈ [r]).

(This is a permutation: (τ_i(s), i) determines (i, s).)  For 0 ≤ ε < 1/2 with εr ∈ ℤ let
𝓕(r,h,ε) := {π_τ : |Fix(τ_i)| ≥ (1−ε)r for every i}.  For τ_i = id this is the tilted grid of [HK];
in general each τ_i permutes the value blocks visited by position block i.

**Lemma 9 (𝓕 ⊆ 𝓛_r).**  For every τ, π_τ^{-1} is increasing on each value interval V_j = [jh, (j+1)h).
*Proof.*  π_τ^{-1}(jh + i) = i·r + τ_i^{-1}(j) is increasing in i.  ∎

**Lemma 10 (long vertical and horizontal chains for every shift).**  Let π = π_τ ∈ 𝓕(r,h,ε) and write
F_i := Fix(τ_i).  Then for Δ = d·h + e with 0 ≤ d < r, 0 ≤ e < h, Δ ≥ 1,
   (a)  L^V_Δ(π) ≥ max( (h−e)·((1−2ε)r − d),  e·((1−2ε)r − d − 1) ),
and for Δ' = d'·r + e' with 0 ≤ d' < h, 0 ≤ e' < r, Δ' ≥ 1,
   (b)  L^H_{Δ'}(π) ≥ max( (h−d')·((1−2ε)r − e'),  (h−d'−1)·(e' − 2εr) ).
*Proof.*  Write elements as (i,s), with position p(i,s) = ir + s and value v(i,s) = τ_i(s)h + i.
Counting fact: for any i, i' and 0 ≤ u < r, |F_i ∩ (F_{i'} + u)| ≥ (1−2ε)r − u, since F_i ∩ [u, r) and
(F_{i'} ∩ [0, r−u)) + u are subsets of [u, r) (size r − u) each of size ≥ (1−ε)r − u; and
|F_i ∩ [0,u) ∩ (F_{i'} + u − r)| ≥ u − 2εr, since F_i ∩ [0,u) and (F_{i'} ∩ [r−u, r)) + u − r are subsets
of [0,u) each of size ≥ u − εr.
(a) Group 1: i ≥ e.  For s ∈ F_i ∩ (F_{i−e} + d) we have v(i,s) − Δ = (s − d)h + (i − e) = v(i−e, s−d),
because τ_{i−e}(s−d) = s−d; so b(i,s) = (i−e, s−d), i.e. b is the translation p ↦ p − (er + d) on
A_1 := {(i,s) : i ≥ e, s ∈ F_i ∩ (F_{i−e}+d)}, hence increasing: A_1 is a vertical Δ-chain of size
≥ (h−e)((1−2ε)r − d).  Group 2: i < e (needs e ≥ 1).  For s ∈ F_i ∩ (F_{i−e+h} + d + 1):
v(i,s) − Δ = (s−d−1)h + (i − e + h) = v(i−e+h, s−d−1), so b is the translation p ↦ p + (h−e)r − d − 1
on A_2 := {(i,s) : i < e, s ∈ F_i ∩ (F_{i−e+h} + d + 1)}, a chain of size ≥ e((1−2ε)r − d − 1).
(b) Group 1: i ≥ d', s ≥ e'.  For s ∈ F_i ∩ (F_{i−d'} + e'): p(i,s) − Δ' = p(i−d', s−e') and
v(i−d', s−e') = (s−e')h + (i−d') = v(i,s) − (e'h + d').  So on X_1 := {(i,s) : i ≥ d', s ∈ F_i ∩ (F_{i−d'}+e')}
the map x ↦ π(x−Δ') equals π(x) − (e'h + d'), comonotone with π: X_1 is a horizontal Δ'-chain of size
≥ (h−d')((1−2ε)r − e').  Group 2: i ≥ d'+1, s < e'.  For s ∈ F_i ∩ [0,e') ∩ (F_{i−d'−1} + e' − r):
p(i,s) − Δ' = (i−d'−1)r + (s − e' + r) = p(i−d'−1, s−e'+r) and v(i−d'−1, s−e'+r) = v(i,s) + (r−e')h − d' − 1,
again comonotone; size ≥ (h−d'−1)(e' − 2εr).  ∎

**Theorem 11 (the family defeats every dihedral image).**  Let k = rh with r, h ≥ 20, ε ≤ 1/16, εr ∈ ℤ.
Then for every π ∈ 𝓕(r,h,ε), every g ∈ D and every Δ ∈ [1, 0.7k]:  L_Δ(π^g) ≥ k/16.
Consequently, for every threshold L ≤ k/16 there do not exist three shifts 0 ≤ t_1 < t_2 < t_3 ≤ k with
all pairwise differences outside F := {Δ : L_Δ(π^g) > L}; in particular no image π^g satisfies the
hypothesis of [W9] Theorem 8, of [W13] Theorem 5 or Corollary 6 with ℓ ≥ 3 threads, or of Theorem 8
above with ℓ ≥ 3 threads (all of which require |F| < k/(2ℓ) ≤ k/6 < 0.7k), and for ℓ = 2 they require
|F| < k/4, which also fails.
*Proof.*  By Lemma 1 it suffices to treat L^V_Δ(π) and L^H_Δ(π).  Vertical: Δ ≤ 0.7k gives
d = ⌊Δ/h⌋ ≤ 0.7r, so (1−2ε)r − d − 1 ≥ (7/8 − 0.7)r − 1 = 0.175r − 1 ≥ r/8 (r ≥ 20), and
max(h−e, e) ≥ h/2; Lemma 10(a) gives L^V_Δ ≥ (h/2)(r/8) = k/16.  Horizontal: d' = ⌊Δ/r⌋ ≤ 0.7h, so
h − d' − 1 ≥ 0.3h − 1 ≥ h/4 (h ≥ 20); the two bounds in Lemma 10(b) are ≥ (h/4)·((7/8)r − e') and
(h/4)·(e' − r/8), whose maximum is at least their average (h/4)(3r/8) ≥ k/16.  For the consequence: three
shifts in [0,k] have two consecutive gaps summing to ≤ k, so one gap is ≤ k/2 < 0.7k and lies in F.  The
greedy hypothesis ℓ(2|F|+1) ≤ k of [W9]/[W13] needs |F| < k/(2ℓ), while |F| ≥ 0.7k.  ∎

**Proposition 12 (size).**  |𝓕(r,h,ε)| ≥ exp( εk·ln((1−ε)r) − h ln 3 ).  With r = h = √k and ε = 1/16
this is ≥ exp( (1/32)·k ln k − O(k) ).
*Proof.*  π_τ determines τ (τ_i(s) = ⌊π_τ(ir+s)/h⌋), so |𝓕| = N^h where N is the number of τ_i ∈ S_r with
≥ (1−ε)r fixed points.  Choosing the fixed-point set (C(r, εr) ways) and a derangement of the
complement (D_{εr} ≥ (εr)!/3 for εr ≥ 1) gives distinct permutations, so
N ≥ C(r,εr)(εr)!/3 = r!/(3((1−ε)r)!) ≥ ((1−ε)r)^{εr}/3.  ∎

**Corollary 13 (what this rules out).**  (i) By Lemma 9 and Theorem 11, 𝓕(r,h,ε) ⊆ 𝓛_r consists of
patterns for which at most two shifts can be chosen; so the entropy lemma (E) proposed in [W13] log.md §4
("#{π ∈ 𝓛_r : at most j shifts choosable} ≤ e^{(29j−1)k}") is false for j = 2 as soon as
εk ln((1−ε)r) − h ln 3 > 57k, e.g. for ε = 1/16, r = h = √k, k > e^{1830}.  More generally no
bound of the form Pr(σ ⊅ π) ≤ e^{−Dk} with D independent of k (single thread, or any bounded number of
threads at n = O(k²)) can be union-bounded over 𝓕(√k, √k, 1/16), because |𝓕| ≥ e^{(1/32 − o(1))k ln k}.
(ii) The residual class of the symmetric method — patterns none of whose eight images satisfies the
