**The literature baseline needs an immediate correction.**

Altschuler–Dubroff–Tikhomirov's August 2026 paper gives online embedding concentration (Theorem 1.3), a worst-target limiting constant at most 0.50568 (Theorem 1.5), and typical-target constant at most 0.49967 with concentration over targets (Theorem 1.7). Consequently, for any fixed ε>0, its results imply offline containment with high probability at (0.50568+ε)k² uniformly for individual targets, and at (0.49967+ε)k² for a random target. The deduction is to standardize the initial iid stream; a Θ(k²) margin dominates the stated fluctuations. This supersedes W19's 0.757 and W29's 0.527 benchmarks, but leaves W31's 0.465 candidate competitive. It does not imply simultaneous universality. The priority note's dismissal of this paper as unrelated to superpattern length misses these consequences. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19050v1).

The general random-superpattern upper bound remains O(k² log log k) in the primary literature checked for this review. [He–Kwan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12878). For the deterministic lower-bound comparison, use the precise published Chroman–Kwan–Singhal coefficient, 1.000076, rather than a rounded baseline. **A concrete deduction to write up next: shared boxes for arbitrary monotone-block inflations.**

This strengthens the defect-free part of W37 and illustrates the right mechanism. It is a deduction from classical LIS tails, not a novelty claim; check prior art before presenting it as a new theorem.

Fix ε>0. There is K_ε such that a uniform permutation of length ceil((1/4+ε)k²), with high probability, simultaneously contains every length-k permutation obtained by inflating an arbitrary permutation by increasing or decreasing blocks, each of length at least K_ε sqrt(log k). An inflation means that each block occupies consecutive positions and a consecutive interval of values, and the value-order of the blocks is prescribed by the arbitrary smaller permutation. Direct and skew sums of long monotone runs are special cases.

Here is the argument. Choose C strictly between 1/4 and 1/4+ε and work with a Poisson process of intensity Ck² on the unit square. For integer s,t,a with 0≤s,t≤k−a and a≥L, consider

    Q(s,t,a) = (s/k,(s+a)/k) × (t/k,(t+a)/k).

There are at most (k+1)³ such squares. Inside one square the expected number of points is Ca². The standard LIS lower-tail estimate, together with reflection for decreasing subsequences, gives a constant c_C>0 such that the probability it lacks an increasing or a decreasing subsequence of length a is at most 2 exp(−c_C a²), for all sufficiently large a. This uses the lower-tail large-deviation estimate at a fixed ratio strictly below the typical LIS length, including the standard Poissonization step. [Deuschel–Zeitouni](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9803035).

Thus the probability that any of these squares fails is at most

    2(k+1)³ exp(−c_C L²).

For an inflation with block lengths a_i, take s_i to be the cumulative length of preceding blocks in position order and t_i to be the cumulative length of blocks below block i in value order. Its block fits in Q(s_i,t_i,a_i). On the common event, select the appropriate monotone subsequence in each square. Their x-intervals and y-intervals enforce exactly the required block order, so their union is the target inflation. This works simultaneously for every skeleton, every composition, and every choice of block directions. Taking L=ceil(K sqrt(log k)) with c_C K²>3 makes the failure probability vanish. Coupling to a fixed host size ceil((1/4+ε)k²) adds only the probability that the Poisson count exceeds that size, which is exp(−Ω_ε(k²)).

For direct/skew sums alone, only O(k²) diagonal or antidiagonal squares are needed. The more general O(k³) family above costs no change in the order of the minimum block length. This improves W37's sqrt(k) log k requirement for its defect-free subclass. It does not cover bounded-size blocks such as repeated 21, general interleaved grids, or typical random targets. The next difficulty is precisely allowing such interleaving while retaining a manageable family of common certificates.
